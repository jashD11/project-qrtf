The story so far02 / 15

How the strategy trades

  • Every day a machine-learning model ranks ~500 Indian stocks by expected return over the next few days.
  • Buy the top tenth. Sell short the bottom tenth, but only stocks that can actually be borrowed.
  • Three portfolio styles: long-only, long/short, and 130/30 (130% long, 30% short; market-neutral when markets are stressed).
  • The model is refitted every quarter on the past two years, and only ever scored on days it has not seen.

Schematic: one day's 500 stocks, ordered by the model's predicted return.

The story so far03 / 15

Five phases, one question: does it survive real costs?

  • Each phase removed one comfortable assumption: simulated prices, free trading, zero bid-ask spread, shorting anything, counting only the attempts that worked.
  • Each correction was applied to every strategy alike, whether it helped or hurt.
  • The luck test: when many variants are tried, the best looks good partly by chance. A result passes only if it is 95% likely to beat the best that luck alone would produce (the Deflated Sharpe Ratio).
The story so far04 / 15

Where we stood: the forecast was fading

  • Forecast skill = each day, the rank correlation between the model's ordering of stocks and how they actually did. 0 is a coin flip; 0.02–0.05 is useful in equities.
  • 2016–20 it averaged 0.071. From 2021 it fell to 0.029, and to 0.015 in 2024–25.
  • Phase 5's smarter trading (swap only when the gain beats the cost) lifted the best strategy to 0.96, short of the 1.8 needed. What's left is the forecast.
  • That is what Phase 6 set out to explain, and fix.

Mean daily rank correlation between forecast and the next 5 days' returns. Dashed lines: day-weighted averages.

Q1 · Why the edge faded06 / 15

What faded, and what held

  • Faded (red): low volatility, lottery-like jumps, illiquidity, 1-year momentum. About 59% of their strength is gone.
  • Held (green): short-term reversal and turnover. Some got stronger.
  • Five causes were tested against pass marks set in advance (appendix A2). None met its mark; crowding missed by one signal (7 of 11 faded, 8 needed).
  • The faded group is the most published set of stock anomalies, and published anomalies lose about half their return. Reading: they got crowded out.

Low volatility: Ang et al. (2006), Frazzini & Pedersen (2014). Lottery: Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw (2011). Illiquidity: Amihud (2002). Decay after publication: McLean & Pontiff (2016).

Q1 · Why the edge faded07 / 15

No model could have found it, not even one that cheats

Forecast skill by year, 5-day forecast. 2025 is January–June.

  • The “cheating” model is fitted inside each year, on that year's own future. In 2024–25 even it finds about −0.001.
  • A simple linear model fades exactly like the trees, so it is not the choice of learner.
  • A frozen rule, just each signal's 2016–20 direction, never updated, beats the trees from 2021 on: 0.047 vs 0.029.
  • So: change the information, not the model.
Presenter note. The cheating model trains on about 190 days against the real model's 504, so it sits below the real model even in early years. It is built the same way every year, and its collapse is what matters.
Q2 · Trying to improve it08 / 15

The plan: richer information, cheaper trading

  • Richer information: three nested sets, so each layer's value can be read off by itself.
  • The new data was chosen to be different from what faded: what option traders and futures markets are pricing, how stocks move with their sector and the market.
  • Cheaper trading: the 21-day forecast is rebalanced once a month instead of daily.
  • The grid: 3 data sets × 2 forecasts × 3 styles = 18 strategies, all trading with M2. A pass needs about 1.7 after costs.

Takeaways12 / 15

What we learned

The edge most likely faded because it got crowded. More of the same kind of data can't buy it back.

  • Why it faded: the crowded low-volatility signals lost about 59% of their strength. Short-term reversal held. No model, not even one that sees the future, recovers it.
  • The honest baseline is lower than we thought: 0.78 after fixing a small training leak, not 0.96.
  • Monthly rebalancing cut trading cost to 3–5% a year, but gave up more return than it saved.
  • New data made things worse: forecast skill roughly halved, and all 6 strategies lost Sharpe. The good new signals were diluted in the mix.
  • Next: genuinely new information (delivery) or a different source of return, each tested once on the sealed data.

Every rule and threshold was fixed before any result was seen, along with a decision to stop adding price-based data if nothing passed. Nothing passed, so it stopped.
